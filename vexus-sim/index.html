Hepatic vein

  • A and V are retrograde (above baseline); S and D are antegrade (below).
  • A follows the P wave, S follows the QRS, V is end systole, D follows the T wave.
  • Normal S > D. Mild: S < D, still below baseline. Severe: S reversed above baseline.
  • Pitfalls: AF causes S < D without high RAP. Cirrhosis blunts phasicity. Structural TR keeps S reversed even after decongestion.

Portal vein

  • Normal flow is continuous and toward the probe (red), because sinusoids dampen RAP.
  • PVPF = (Vmax − Vmin) / Vmax × 100.
  • Under 30% normal, 30 to 50% mild, 50% or more severe.
  • Pitfalls: lean people and athletes can be pulsatile normally; cirrhosis can raise or mask pulsatility.

Intrarenal vein

  • Use interlobar veins, abdominal preset, scale below 20 cm/s.
  • Normal: continuous. Mild: biphasic with distinct S and D. Severe: monophasic, D only.
  • The arterial waveform above baseline times the cardiac cycle.
  • Hardest view to obtain; not studied in CKD or renal transplant.

Grading

  • IVC 2 cm or less: grade 0, Doppler not graded.
  • IVC over 2 cm and no severe waveform: grade 1.
  • One severe waveform: grade 2. Two or more: grade 3.
  • VExUS shows congestion, not volume responsiveness. Interpret with cardiac POCUS and the clinical picture.
